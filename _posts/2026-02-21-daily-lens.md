## 🔥 Top Story: Supreme Court Strikes Down Trump's Tariffs

**The Ruling:** In a historic decision Friday, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that President Trump's sweeping tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEPPA) are unconstitutional. Chief Justice John Roberts, writing for the majority, declared that Trump lacked the peacetime authority to use IEPPA for tariff implementation.

**What Survives:** Notably, auto tariffs remain in place—those were enacted under different statutory authority. But the "reciprocal" tariffs that defined Trump's trade policy are now legally dead.

**The Fallout:**
- Companies are lining up for *billions* in refunds on tariffs already paid
- Trump slammed the justices immediately after the ruling
- The administration signaled it's exploring "other avenues" to reimpose levies
- Markets will digest this Monday—expect volatility in trade-sensitive sectors

**Why It Matters:** This is the first time the Supreme Court has set a hard limit on Trump's power after siding with him in two dozen cases over the past year. The court's conservative majority finally found a line it wouldn't cross.

**Tickers to Watch:** Import-heavy retailers (WMT, TGT, COST), semiconductor supply chains (TSM, INTC), and steel/aluminum plays that benefited from protection (X, NUE, AA).

## 🚗 Tesla Cybercab Production Begins at Gigafactory Texas

**The News:** The first Cybercab just rolled off the production line at Tesla's Gigafactory Texas. Elon Musk confirmed that consumer versions will sell for **$30,000 or less** this year.

**What Is It:** A fully autonomous vehicle with no pedals and no steering wheel. This isn't a car you drive—it's a car that drives you.

**The Play:**
- Tesla enters the robotaxi market in direct competition with Waymo
- $30K price point makes it accessible beyond fleet operators
- Production in April means deliveries likely by Q3

**Market Implication:** This validates Tesla's pivot from "car company" to "autonomy company." Watch for pressure on ride-share names (UBER, LYFT) as the robotaxi reality approaches.

**Ticker:** TSLA

## ✈️ NORAD Intercepts Russian Aircraft Near Alaska

**The Incident:** Five Russian military aircraft, including Su-35 fighters, were intercepted by NORAD F-16s near the Bering Strait on Thursday. The U.S. military stated there was no direct threat, but the interceptions highlight ongoing tensions in Arctic airspace.

**Context:** This comes as Iranian naval drills continue in the Hormuz Strait and the U.S. plans increased missile and drone deployments to the Philippines. The defense posture is escalating on multiple fronts.

**Defense Sector Note:** The Air Force is restocking GBU-57 "bunker buster" bombs used against Iranian nuclear facilities last June. The Sentinel ICBM program also reported "considerable progress" in the last 12-18 months.

**Tickers:** Defense contractors remain in focus—LMT, RTX, NOC, GD.

## 🎬 Disney Sends Cease & Desist to ByteDance Over AI Content

**The Conflict:** Disney has sent a legal warning to ByteDance over "Seedance 2.0," the TikTok parent company's new video-generation AI. Disney alleges the AI was trained on or outputs content featuring Disney characters without authorization.

**Why It's Big:** This is the first major copyright battle between a legacy media giant and a Chinese tech firm over AI-generated content. It sets the stage for broader fights over training data and IP rights in generative AI.

**The Precedent:** How this plays out could influence every company training AI on internet content. If Disney wins, expect more aggressive enforcement from content owners globally.

**Tickers:** DIS, entertainment sector; also watch AI platform plays (META, GOOGL, MSFT) for regulatory exposure.

## 💾 Micron Surges on AI Memory Shortage

**The Stat:** MU is up **44% year-to-date**, driven by an acute shortage of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) needed for AI accelerators.

**The Story:** Every AI chip needs memory to move data. NVIDIA's GPUs, AMD's MI300X, and custom chips from Google and Amazon all need HBM from Micron and SK Hynix. Supply can't keep up with demand.

**Analyst View:** One investor called it "a fork in the road"—either memory margins stay elevated for years, or oversupply returns. Current pricing power suggests the former.

**Ticker:** MU

## 🧠 One Thought

The Supreme Court ruling isn't just about tariffs. It's about the limits of executive power in economic policy. For a year, courts rubber-stamped emergency justifications. Yesterday they didn't. 

Markets priced in tariff uncertainty for months. Now they get a different uncertainty: what does Trump do next? "Other avenues" is vague, but this president doesn't accept defeat quietly.

Monday should be interesting.
