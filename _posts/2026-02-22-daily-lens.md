## 🔥 Top Story: Trump Escalates to 15% Global Tariffs

**The Response:** Less than 24 hours after the Supreme Court struck down his emergency tariffs, President Trump announced a new global tariff of 10%—then immediately raised it to 15%. The message is clear: the court may have ruled against him, but he's not backing down.

**The Legal Angle:** These new tariffs reportedly use different statutory authority than the IEEPA-based levies the Supreme Court rejected Friday. The administration is betting it can find constitutional ground to stand on while companies line up for refunds on the old tariffs.

**What's Different:**
- 15% across-the-board on all imports (vs. targeted "reciprocal" rates before)
- Trump called the court "unpatriotic and disloyal to our Constitution"
- Markets will digest this Monday—expect trade-sensitive sectors to whipsaw

**Why It Matters:** The constitutional showdown isn't over. Trump is testing whether the court's ruling was a blanket prohibition or a narrow procedural objection. Either way, the uncertainty premium is back in markets.

**Tickers to Watch:** Importers (WMT, TGT, COST, AMZN), autos (F, GM), semiconductors (TSM, INTC, NVDA), steel/aluminum (X, NUE, AA).

## 📉 US Investors Flee at Fastest Pace in 16 Years

**The Numbers:** U.S. investors are pulling money out of domestic equities at the fastest pace since at least 2010, according to fund flow data. The culprit? Fading Big Tech returns and increasingly attractive opportunities overseas.

**Where It's Going:**
- European equities seeing significant inflows (STOXX up 0.84%)
- Emerging markets getting renewed attention
- The "US exceptionalism" trade is showing cracks

**The Context:** After years of outperformance, the Magnificent Seven are no longer carrying the market. Meanwhile, European markets are cheaper, Chinese stimulus is ramping up, and the tariff chaos adds domestic risk.

**Market Data (Friday close):**
- S&P 500: 6,909.51 (+0.69%)
- Nasdaq: 22,886.07 (+0.90%)
- Gold: $5,071/oz (+1.91%) — flight to safety continues
- EUR/USD: 1.1780 — dollar weakness persists

**What to Watch:** If outflows accelerate, expect continued dollar weakness and multiple compression in US large-caps. The "rest of world" trade may have legs.

## 🇮🇷 Iran Student Protests Erupt for First Time Since Crackdown

**The Scene:** Iranian university students staged the first large-scale anti-government protests since security forces killed thousands in last month's demonstrations. The protests coincided with mourning ceremonies for those who died.

**The Tension:** This comes as the U.S. and Iran have resumed nuclear negotiations while simultaneously the U.S. builds up military capability in the Middle East. The administration hasn't explained its case for potential military action to the American public.

**Defense Angle:** The dual-track approach—diplomacy and military buildup—suggests contingency planning for various outcomes. Defense contractors remain relevant.

**Tickers:** Defense sector (LMT, RTX, NOC), oil hedges if regional tensions escalate (XLE, USO).

## 🏔️ Lake Tahoe Avalanche: Final Victim Found, 9 Dead

**The Tragedy:** The final missing skier from Tuesday's avalanche near Lake Tahoe was found dead Saturday, bringing the total to nine fatalities—six clients and three guides. It's the deadliest avalanche in California history.

**The Victims:** Eight backcountry skiers were found over five days of searching in brutal conditions. The group was experienced, with professional guides, but the mountain doesn't discriminate.

**Broader Context:** This follows an unusually warm winter with unstable snowpack across the Sierra Nevada. Climate-driven snow volatility is changing risk calculations for backcountry recreation.

## 🏅 Winter Olympics: Klaebo Makes History, Team GB Takes 5th Medal

**The Record:** Norway's Johannes Hoesflot Klaebo won his sixth gold medal of these Games, breaking the all-time record for most gold medals at a single Winter Olympics. The cross-country skiing legend cemented his legacy on the final day in Milan-Cortina.

**Team GB:** Zoe Atkin won bronze in the women's freeski halfpipe, giving Great Britain its fifth medal and matching their best-ever Winter Olympics haul.

**Final Event:** The men's ice hockey final features Canada vs. USA—a politically charged matchup given current trade tensions. Canada won men's curling gold on Saturday, denying Team GB.

**The Spectacle:** This Olympics featured everything from a curling cheating scandal to viral human-interest stories. The Games close tonight in Milan.

## 🎭 Eric Dane, 'Grey's Anatomy' Star and ALS Advocate, Dies at 53

**The Loss:** Eric Dane, best known as "McSteamy" on Grey's Anatomy, died Friday at 53. In recent years, he became an outspoken ALS advocate after a family member's diagnosis.

**The Career:** Beyond Grey's, Dane starred in The Last Ship and X-Men: The Last Stand. He was open about his mental health struggles, using his platform to reduce stigma.

**The Legacy:** His ALS advocacy raised millions and brought attention to research funding gaps. The ALS Association released a statement honoring his "tireless work."

## 🚀 NASA Targets March 6 for Artemis II Moon Mission

**The Update:** NASA officials confirmed Friday that Artemis II—the first crewed mission to the moon since 1972—is targeting March 6 for launch. The agency overcame rocket-fueling issues in a second successful rehearsal this week.

**The Mission:** Four astronauts will orbit the moon and return to Earth. It's a precursor to Artemis III, which will land humans on the lunar surface.

**The Caveat:** NASA cautioned that remaining prep work could warrant more time. Space launch schedules slip regularly, but the program is making real progress.

## 🧠 One Thought

Meanwhile, the bigger story might be buried in fund flows. US investors voting with their feet, moving money overseas at the fastest pace in over a decade. When the home team starts betting against itself, that's worth paying attention to.

The "America First" trade may be losing believers.
