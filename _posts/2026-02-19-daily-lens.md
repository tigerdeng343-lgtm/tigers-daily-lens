## 👑 Prince Andrew Arrested on Suspicion of Misconduct in Public Office

The most explosive royal news since the abdication.

**What happened:** Prince Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor was arrested this morning at the Sandringham Estate in Norfolk. Thames Valley Police confirmed a man is in custody on suspicion of "misconduct in public office." Officers are searching addresses in both Berkshire and Norfolk.

**The charge:** "Misconduct in public office" is a serious criminal offense in the UK—it carries a maximum sentence of life imprisonment. It requires proving that a public official willfully neglected their duty or acted corruptly while holding public office.

**The context:** Andrew's troubles have been well-documented—his friendship with Jeffrey Epstein, the Virginia Giuffre lawsuit settlement, and his fall from royal grace. But arrest and custody is an entirely different magnitude of consequence.

**The royal response:** Buckingham Palace has not yet commented. The arrest at Sandringham, a royal residence, is deeply symbolic—police entering the heart of the monarchy itself.

**Why it matters for markets:**
- Limited direct market impact, but watch UK political stability narrative
- Any involvement of other prominent figures could cascade
- The Epstein connection continues generating consequences years later

## 🇰🇷 Yoon Suk Yeol: Life in Prison for Martial Law Insurrection

South Korea's former president receives the maximum sentence.

**The verdict:** A Seoul court sentenced former President Yoon Suk Yeol to life in prison for masterminding an insurrection linked to his December 2024 martial law declaration.

**The backstory:** Yoon stunned the world on December 3, 2024 when he declared martial law, deploying soldiers to the National Assembly. Politicians climbed walls to enter and vote down the decree. Within hours, it collapsed—but the constitutional crisis lasted months.

**The sentence:** Life imprisonment is the maximum for insurrection under South Korean law. Yoon, who rose from prosecutor to president in just one year in politics, now faces spending his remaining years behind bars.

**What colleagues say in hindsight:** There were early warning signs, according to those who worked with him. A prosecutor who used his career as a springboard, moving faster than anyone expected—and falling harder.

**Market implications:**
- Political uncertainty resolved, in a way—the legal drama is concluding
- South Korean markets (EWY) may see reduced political risk premium
- Korean tech (Samsung, SK Hynix) can focus on execution rather than political noise
- Won stability could improve

**The historical note:** This is the third South Korean president to face criminal conviction—following Park Geun-hye and Lee Myung-bak. Democracy, Korean-style, holds its leaders accountable.

## 📱 Zuckerberg Defends Meta in Landmark Social Media Addiction Trial

The billionaire CEO takes the stand in a case that could reshape tech.

**The trial:** Mark Zuckerberg testified in a landmark lawsuit alleging Meta's platforms (Facebook, Instagram) are addictive and harmful to children. This is one of the most significant tech trials in years.

**His defense:** Zuckerberg said he "always regretted not making faster progress" in identifying users under 13. He defended Meta's systems while acknowledging room for improvement.

**The context:** Instagram's Adam Mosseri testified earlier, saying he doesn't believe people can get "clinically addicted" to social media—a statement that drew immediate criticism from parents' groups and mental health advocates.

**What's at stake:**
- **For Meta:** Potential billions in liability, forced platform changes
- **For the industry:** This trial could establish precedent for holding platforms accountable for algorithmic engagement
- **For regulation:** A plaintiff victory could accelerate legislative action

**The market read:**
- **META** has largely shrugged off legal risk—up 0.56% yesterday
- Market currently pricing low probability of material financial impact
- But adverse ruling could change the calculus quickly

**The bigger picture:** We're watching the beginning of social media's "tobacco moment"—when society decided the health costs outweighed the profits. The trial's outcome matters less than the cultural shift it represents.

## 💰 Etsy Sells Depop to eBay for $1.2 Billion

Five years, and a different owner.

**The deal:** Etsy is selling Depop, the "pre-loved" fashion marketplace, to eBay for $1.2 billion. Etsy bought Depop for $1.6 billion in 2021.

**The math:** That's a $400 million haircut, but Etsy is refocusing on its core marketplace.

**Why eBay wants it:** Depop's Gen-Z user base and fashion credibility fill a gap in eBay's aging platform. It's a youth infusion.

**Why Etsy is selling:** Depop struggled to scale profitably under Etsy's ownership. Strategic focus on handmade/vintage core.

**The market read:**
- **ETSY:** Shareholders may prefer focused execution over diversification
- **EBAY:** Betting on resale fashion trend's durability
- **Broader:** The secondhand economy is real, but monetizing it remains challenging

**Watchlist:** ETSY, EBAY, REAL (luxury resale), TDUP (ThredUp)

## 🎿 California Avalanche: Eight Skiers Dead, One Missing

Tragedy in Lake Tahoe's backcountry.

**The toll:** Eight backcountry skiers have been found dead following a massive avalanche near Lake Tahoe on Tuesday. One person remains missing and is presumed dead. Fifteen skiers were caught in the slide.

**The rescue:** Teams deployed from nearby ski resorts, racing against time to reach six trapped skiers and nine who ultimately didn't survive. The terrain and conditions made rescue extraordinarily difficult.

**The context:** This is one of the deadliest avalanche events in US history. Backcountry skiing has grown enormously popular post-COVID, but so has risk exposure.

**Climate connection:** Unstable snowpacks from variable temperatures create dangerous conditions. Warmer winters followed by cold snaps increase avalanche risk.

**No market implication**—but a reminder that nature remains humbling and deadly.
