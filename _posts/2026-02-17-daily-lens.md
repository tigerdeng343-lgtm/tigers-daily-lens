## ✊ Jesse Jackson (1941-2026): The Voice That Carried On

The civil rights giant has died at 84.

**The man:** Rev. Jesse L. Jackson Sr., protégé of Martin Luther King Jr., two-time presidential candidate, and arguably the most influential civil rights leader of the post-MLK era, died peacefully Tuesday morning surrounded by his family.

**Why he mattered:** Jackson was there at the Lorraine Motel when King was assassinated in 1968. He didn't just carry the movement forward—he expanded it. Operation PUSH. The Rainbow Coalition. Running for president in 1984 and 1988, winning primaries and proving a Black candidate could compete nationally. He built the infrastructure that eventually elected Barack Obama.

**The complicated legacy:** Jackson was a masterful organizer and orator who could unite disparate coalitions—hence "Rainbow Coalition." He registered millions of voters. He negotiated hostage releases abroad. He also attracted controversy, from business dealings to personal conduct. He contained multitudes.

**The Parkinson's years:** Jackson announced his Parkinson's diagnosis in 2017. In recent years, his public appearances became rarer, his famous voice diminished. But he remained engaged until the end.

**What's lost:** Jackson represented a style of activism—confrontational but coalition-building, morally grounded but politically pragmatic—that feels rare today. He could march and negotiate in the same week.

**The context:** He died as civil rights faces new challenges: voting restrictions, affirmative action rollbacks, a more polarized racial discourse. The movement he shaped must adapt without him.

Rest in power.

## 🕊️ Iran-US Nuclear Talks: Day One in Geneva

After weeks of buildup, diplomacy begins.

**What happened:** Direct talks between the US and Iran commenced in Geneva today. This is the first serious nuclear negotiation since the JCPOA collapsed in 2018. Both sides describe the atmosphere as "serious but constructive"—diplomatic speak for "difficult but not walking out."

**The backdrop:** As I wrote yesterday, the US has two aircraft carrier groups positioned near Iran. That's not posturing—it's capability positioning. The military pressure is real and both sides know it.

**Iran's position:** Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi met with the IAEA chief yesterday, signaling Iran's willingness to engage on verification issues. Iran wants sanctions relief, full stop. Its economy is strangled.

**US position:** The Trump administration wants a deal they can call tougher than JCPOA. They're pushing for longer sunset clauses and missile program limits. Whether Iran accepts this is the question.

**Why both sides might deal:** Iran's economy can't sustain current sanctions indefinitely. The US doesn't want another Middle East war while juggling other global priorities. A narrow deal that freezes enrichment for partial sanctions relief could work.

**What could blow it up:** Hardliners on both sides have incentives to sabotage talks. Watch for provocative actions—military incidents, terror attacks, inflammatory statements—designed to derail progress.

**Market read:** Markets reopen today. If talks go well, oil dips and risk assets rally. Defense names might give back some gains. If talks falter, opposite reaction. This is a headline-driven week.

**The plays:** Long: XOM, CVX, OXY if talks fail. Short-term calls on RTX, LMT if escalation headlines hit. Patient: wait for clarity before sizing positions.

## 🇺🇦 Russia-Ukraine: Talks Continue, So Does Bombing

Peace negotiations happen while infrastructure burns.

**The pattern:** Third round of Russia-Ukraine talks scheduled this week. Meanwhile, Russian forces pounded Ukrainian power infrastructure overnight, killing three energy workers and leaving tens of thousands without power and heat.

**What it means:** Russia is negotiating from a position of continued pressure. They won't stop military operations just because diplomats are meeting. This is designed to weaken Ukraine's position at the table.

**Ukraine's demographic crisis:** BBC reports Ukraine is now paying for soldiers to freeze their eggs and sperm. The country faces a "demographic catastrophe"—its population has cratered due to casualties, emigration, and collapsing birth rates. This war's costs extend far beyond the battlefield.

**The negotiation reality:** Any deal will be ugly. Ukraine won't get back all its territory. Russia won't get all its demands. Both sides will claim victory while accepting painful compromises. The question is whether either domestic audience will accept the outcome.

**Market implications:** European defense remains a secular long regardless of peace talks. Even a ceasefire doesn't end Europe's rearmament drive—Germany and others have decided to rebuild military capability independent of this conflict's outcome.

**The plays:** RHM.DE (Rheinmetall), BAE Systems (BA.L), and European defense ETFs remain buy-on-dips candidates.

## 🤖 China's Robot Army: 700 Million Viewers Watched the Future

The Spring Festival Gala wasn't just entertainment—it was a statement.

**What happened:** Four Chinese robotics companies performed at CCTV's Spring Festival Gala on Lunar New Year Eve. Over 700 million people—roughly equivalent to the US and EU combined—watched humanoid robots do martial arts, backflips, and comedy sketches.

**The companies:** Unitree (Hangzhou), Magiclab (Wuxi), Galbot (Beijing), and Noetix all showcased advanced robotics capabilities. Unitree's robots did martial arts with backflips and trampoline leaps. This wasn't CGI—these were real machines.

**The CEO quote that matters:** Unitree's Wang Xingxing told CCTV that if embodied AI breakthroughs happen, the sector could be "100 or even 1,000 times" bigger than current levels, "far surpassing the mobile internet era."

**Why this matters for markets:** China is signaling robotics as a national priority. Their 15th Five-Year Plan identifies embodied intelligence as a growth engine. Unitree already sells humanoid robots for $16K. Tesla's Optimus now has real competition.

## 🥌 Winter Olympics: The Curling Scandal Nobody Expected

Cheating accusations shake Canada's identity sport.

**The situation:** Both Canadian curling teams—men's and women's—face cheating allegations at the Milan Cortina Games. The sport known for its honor code (players traditionally call their own fouls) is having an existential moment.

**What's alleged:** Details are emerging of "swearing, illicit filming, and rule changes." The investigation is ongoing, but Canada's curling reputation—they're traditionally the sport's dominant nation—is taking a hit.

**The Canadian response:** "A sad day," according to BBC's coverage of Canadian reaction. For a country where curling is practically a national religion, this cuts deep.

**The bigger Olympics picture:** Matt Weston's double skeleton gold for Britain, Norway's cross-country dominance (Klæbo now has nine golds), and Lindsey Vonn's recovery from her crash are the actual sporting stories. But scandals get headlines.

**Vonn update:** She's home in the US after four surgeries on her broken leg but "yet to stand" nine days after the crash. Her Olympic dream is over. At 41, returning from injury, she gave everything. Sometimes that's not enough.
