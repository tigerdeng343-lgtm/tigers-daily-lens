## 🇯🇵 Japan's LDP Wins Historic Landslide — Markets Brace for Monday

In one of the most significant political outcomes this year, **Japan's ruling Liberal Democratic Party secured 328 seats** in the lower house election—their best result in 30 years.

This isn't just a domestic story. It's a market-moving, geopolitics-shifting event.

**Why it matters:**
- **Defense spending acceleration:** The landslide gives Prime Minister confidence to push Japan's military buildup aimed at countering China. Defense stocks in Tokyo should see action.
- **Bank of Japan implications:** Political stability often means policy continuity. The yen could see movement as traders price in rate path clarity.
- **US-Japan alliance:** A strong LDP government means a reliable partner for Washington's Indo-Pacific strategy.

**The Monday trade:** Watch Japanese ETFs (EWJ, DXJ) at the open. A landslide of this magnitude removes political uncertainty—usually bullish for Japanese equities.

## 🥇 Gold: The "Safe Haven" Trading Like a Meme Stock

Here's a sentence I didn't expect to write: **Gold is trading like a meme stock.**

The traditional safe-haven asset has been exhibiting unusual volatility, surging alongside—not inverse to—risk assets. 

**What's happening:**
- Flight-to-safety buying as investors hedge against... everything
- Central bank accumulation (China, Russia, others de-dollarizing)
- Retail FOMO on a 52-week high breakout
- Technical momentum traders piling in

**The paradox:** When your safe haven behaves like a speculative asset, what's actually safe anymore? Some are rotating to short-term Treasuries. Others are just accepting that volatility is the new normal.

**Watch:** GLD, GDX for exposure. But recognize this isn't your grandfather's gold trade.

## 📉 Bitcoin Slumps as Tech Valuations Get Questioned

**Bitcoin's slump coincides with broader investor concerns about inflated tech valuations.**

The correlation is telling: when AI hype gets questioned, crypto gets sold. Both are "long duration" bets on future paradigms, and both get crushed when discount rates (real or perceived) rise.

**The Bithumb footnote:** The Korean exchange apologized after a hack—but claimed to have recovered nearly all the stolen bitcoins. Small comfort in an industry where "nearly all" still means someone lost real money.

**The rotation:** Per Yahoo Finance, investors are chasing smaller, cheaper companies as risk aversion hits the tech sector. When big tech looks like IBM in the 1960s (powerful but overextended), capital flows elsewhere.

## 📰 Washington Post Publisher Steps Down After Layoffs

**Will Lewis, publisher of the Washington Post, abruptly stepped down**—just days after massive layoffs gutted a third of the newspaper's staff.

The timing is suspicious. You don't cut 33% of your workforce and resign immediately unless there's more to the story.

**Media landscape context:** Legacy newspapers continue struggling to find sustainable models. The Post's Bezos-era experimentation hasn't solved the fundamental problem: digital advertising doesn't fund quality journalism.

**For investors:** This is less about any single stock and more about the ongoing media business model crisis. NWSA, NYT, and others face similar existential questions.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand Votes in Three-Way Election

**Thai voters turned out for a general election** defined by a three-way battle between conservative, progressive, and populist camps.
