## 🇨🇦 Canada Reels From Deadliest Mass Shooting in Decades

Nine people are dead and 25 injured after a mass shooting at a school and home in the remote community of Tumbler Ridge, British Columbia. The suspect was found dead at the scene with a self-inflicted injury.

**What happened:** Around 1:20 PM local time Tuesday, an active shooter opened fire at Tumbler Ridge Secondary School, a small institution with just 160 students. Police arrived within two minutes—officials say that response time "no doubt saved lives." Six victims were found inside the school, two at a connected residence, and one died en route to hospital.

**The community:** Tumbler Ridge is a tight-knit town of 2,400 people, 670 km northeast of Vancouver. Mayor Darryl Krakowka told reporters: "I will know every victim. We're a small community... I don't call them residents. I call them family."

Prime Minister Mark Carney cancelled a planned trip to Germany, calling the incident "horrific and devastating." Provincial Premier David Eby urged Canadians to "hug our kids a little bit tighter tonight."

**Why it matters:** This is one of the deadliest mass casualty events in recent Canadian history. The shooter's identity, motive, and connection to the school remain under investigation.

## 📜 Epstein Files Continue to Shake Washington

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick confirmed under oath Tuesday that he visited Jeffrey Epstein's island in 2012—contradicting his previous testimony that he cut ties with the convicted sex offender in 2005.

**The contradiction:** Lutnick previously told Congress he ended contact with Epstein after witnessing inappropriate behavior in 2005. But emails released by the DOJ show his wife Allison wrote to Epstein's assistant in December 2012: "We are looking forward to visiting you... We would love to join you for lunch."

**His defense:** "I did have lunch with him as I was on a boat going across on a family vacation. My wife was with me, as were my four children and nannies... We had lunch on the island. That is true. For an hour."

**The fallout:** Bipartisan calls for Lutnick's resignation have emerged, with both Democratic Rep. Ro Khanna and Republican Rep. Thomas Massie—co-sponsors of the law that compelled the DOJ to release the files—urging him to step down. The White House says he has Trump's "full support."

**Meanwhile:** Ghislaine Maxwell refused to answer questions during her House deposition, repeatedly invoking her right to silence. Lawmakers are now calling on Prince Andrew to testify. The files have named retail billionaire Les Wexner as a potential "co-conspirator" in a 2019 FBI document—though appearing in the files doesn't imply wrongdoing.

## 🇮🇷 Netanyahu Meets Trump as Iran Tensions Escalate

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu arrives at the White House today for his sixth visit since Trump's return to office—more than any other world leader. The meeting comes as US-Iran nuclear talks reach a critical juncture.

**What Netanyahu wants:** A deal that doesn't just halt Iran's uranium enrichment, but also cuts its ballistic missile program and ends support for Hamas and Hezbollah.

**What Iran says:** President Masoud Pezeshkian warned his country would "not yield to excessive demands." An adviser to Supreme Leader Khamenei called missile capabilities a "red line."

**The military backdrop:** The USS Abraham Lincoln carrier strike group is already in the region. Trump told Axios he's "thinking about" sending a second carrier group. He's also threatened strikes if Iran doesn't stop killing protesters.

**Market angle:** Defense stocks benefit from this kind of saber-rattling. Watch **RTX**, **LMT**, and **NOC** for continued strength. Oil got a lift too—Brent crude up 2% to $70.23.

## 🇬🇧 UK Doubles Troops in Norway to Counter Russia

The UK announced it will increase military presence in Norway from 1,000 to 2,000 personnel over the next three years, as part of a new "Arctic Sentry" mission to counter Russian expansion in the region.

**Why now:** Defense Secretary John Healey said Moscow is "rapidly re-establishing military presence in the region, including reopening old Cold War bases." Russian submarine activity in the North Atlantic has risen 30% in two years and is now at Cold War levels.

**What's coming:**
- 1,500 Royal Marine Commandos will participate in NATO's Exercise Cold Response in March
- September brings Operation Lion Protector across Norway, Iceland, and the Danish straits
- Focus on defending undersea cables and pipelines against sabotage

**The bigger picture:** With Trump questioning NATO commitments and Europe scrambling to build defense autonomy, the UK is positioning itself as the reliable transatlantic partner. European defense stocks continue to be a strong sector play—**BAE Systems (BA.L)** and **Rheinmetall (RHM.DE)** remain favorites.
